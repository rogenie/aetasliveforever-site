Biotin for hair: does it work, and the lab-test warning

Biotin is the headline ingredient in many hair, skin and nail supplements. The evidence behind it is much thinner than the shelf space suggests, and there is one safety issue worth knowing about before your next blood test.

By the ÆTAS Research Desk · Reviewed by VITA, our AI editorial reviewer · Checked against sources November 21, 2026 · How we work

Quick answer. For hair loss in people who aren’t biotin deficient, there is no good evidence that biotin helps: a 2024 review found no studies showing a benefit in healthy people. Deficiency is rare in people eating a normal diet, and high-dose biotin can cause falsely high or low results on some blood tests, including heart and thyroid tests, so tell your clinician and lab if you take it.
Review  Evidence at a glance
Best evidence
A 2024 literature review (J Clin Aesthet Dermatol) that found only 3 studies of biotin alone for hair; a 2017 review of 18 case reports (Skin Appendage Disorders)
Women included?
Yes. The only placebo-controlled trial (1966) studied women with diffuse hair loss; another study was 112 women after weight-loss surgery
Dose or protocol tested
The 1966 trial tested 10 mg a day for 4 weeks against placebo; the weight-loss surgery study tested 1 mg a day
Bottom line
Biotin helps hair when there’s a real deficiency or a rare condition. For everyone else, the evidence isn’t there, and the lab-test interference is a real risk

What is biotin, and how much do you need?

Biotin is a water-soluble B vitamin. Your body uses it to process fats, sugars and protein, according to the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements (ODS).

There isn’t enough data to set a full recommended daily allowance, so experts set an “adequate intake” instead:

GroupAdequate intake (per day)
Women 19 and older30 mcg
Pregnancy30 mcg
Breastfeeding35 mcg

Many foods contain some biotin. ODS lists a cooked whole egg at 10 mcg, 3 ounces of canned pink salmon at 5 mcg, a quarter cup of roasted sunflower seeds at 2.6 mcg and half a cup of cooked sweet potato at 2.4 mcg. There are no national US estimates of intake, but ODS says average intakes in other Western countries are about 35 to 70 mcg a day.

ODS notes that no upper limit has been set, because there’s no human evidence that biotin is toxic at high intakes. The bigger concern with high doses is lab tests (see below).

How common is biotin deficiency?

ODS says severe biotin deficiency has never been reported in healthy people eating a normal mixed diet. When deficiency does happen, signs include thinning hair (which can progress to losing all body hair), a scaly red rash around body openings, and brittle nails.

Groups ODS lists as more likely to run low:

  • People with biotinidase deficiency, a rare inherited disorder. All US newborns are screened for it.
  • People with long-term heavy alcohol use, which blocks absorption.
  • Pregnant women. ODS says at least a third of pregnant women develop marginal deficiency.
  • People who eat raw egg whites often. A protein in raw egg white (avidin) binds biotin; cooking breaks it down.

ODS also cites a study of 264 people with epilepsy that linked at least a year of anti-seizure medicine to lower biotin levels. If you take one, ask your prescriber rather than changing anything yourself.

One study complicates the picture. The 2024 review in the Journal of Clinical and Aesthetic Dermatology reports that a 2016 study by Trüeb found low serum biotin in 38% of 541 women with hair loss. But the same review notes that another study found no difference between 60 people with telogen effluvium and 20 controls, and that serum biotin levels are thought to be unreliable. A low lab number isn’t the same as proof that biotin is causing your shedding.

Does biotin work for hair loss? What the studies show

The 2024 review (Yelich and colleagues) searched PubMed for studies of biotin on its own for hair growth or quality, leaving out case reports and case series. Out of 330 search results, only 3 studies qualified:

StudyWhoWhat was testedWhat was found
Pawlowski, 196646 women with diffuse hair loss (28 biotin, 18 placebo)10 mg a day vs placebo, 4 weeks, double-blindNo significant difference in hair growth
Aksac, 202160 people taking isotretinoin (an acne drug that can cause shedding); 30 also got biotin10 mg a day vs no biotin; not blindedA shift toward more hairs in the growing phase on scalp imaging with biotin
Sen, 2021112 women with hair loss after sleeve gastrectomy1 mg a day23% of deficient and 38% of non-deficient women reported improvement; no significant difference between groups

The review found no studies showing that biotin improves hair growth in healthy people. The authors called the evidence low quality. The review reported no funding and no relevant conflicts of interest.

An earlier review in Skin Appendage Disorders (Patel and colleagues, 2017) found 18 reported cases of biotin use for hair and nail changes. In every case, the patient had an underlying condition, such as inherited biotin-processing disorders, a side effect of the seizure drug valproic acid, brittle nail syndrome or uncombable hair syndrome. The authors concluded there wasn’t enough evidence to support biotin for healthy people.

ODS reaches the same place: claims for biotin for hair, skin and nails rest, at best, on a few case reports and small studies. For hair specifically, ODS describes case reports only in children with uncombable hair syndrome.

The biotin lab-test warning: why it matters

Some lab tests use biotin as part of how they work. When there’s a lot of biotin in your blood, it can throw those tests off. FDA says biotin can cause falsely high or falsely low results, depending on the test, which can lead to misdiagnosis. FDA issued safety communications about this on November 28, 2017, and November 5, 2019.

  • Heart tests: FDA says it has continued to receive adverse event reports of falsely low troponin results (troponin is used to diagnose heart attacks). ODS reports that, according to FDA, a patient with a high biotin intake died after a falsely low troponin result.
  • Thyroid tests: ODS describes case reports of results falsely pointing to Graves’ disease and severe hyperthyroidism in people taking 10 to 300 mg a day. Even a single 10 mg dose interfered with thyroid tests run within 24 hours.
  • Other tests: In six healthy adults taking 10 mg a day for a week, some tests gave falsely low TSH (a thyroid test) and NT-proBNP (a heart-failure marker). ODS also lists vitamin D among the tests very high biotin intakes can affect.

For scale, FDA’s October 2020 guidance says the usual 30 mcg daily intake keeps blood biotin below 1 ng/mL, while intakes of up to 300 mg a day produce levels above 1,000 ng/mL. A milligram is 1,000 micrograms, so 10 mg is more than 300 times the adequate intake for adult women.

FDA’s troponin page, current as of June 2022, lists 17 troponin tests subject to biotin interference and says some test makers have addressed it while others have not. ODS says FDA advises clinicians to ask patients about supplements that contain biotin. Ask your clinician or lab whether and when to pause biotin before a test, and don’t skip a test because of it.

Biotin for hair: the honest bottom line

  • If you have a confirmed deficiency or one of the rare conditions above, biotin can help, under your clinician’s care.
  • If you don’t, there’s no good evidence it grows hair, and the one placebo-controlled trial in women found no difference.
  • If you take it anyway, list it on every intake form and tell whoever orders your bloodwork.

Shedding usually has another cause worth finding. Common ones include telogen effluvium, low iron and ferritin, thyroid problems and female pattern hair loss, which has treatments with trial evidence (see minoxidil for women). For an overview, start with hair loss in women: causes and how to tell them apart.

Questions to ask a clinician or board-certified dermatologist:

  • What do you think is causing my hair loss?
  • Is there any reason to test my biotin level, or would other tests tell us more?
  • I take biotin (or a supplement that contains it). Could it affect my upcoming blood tests?
  • Do any of my medicines affect biotin or my hair?
